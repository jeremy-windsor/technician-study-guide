## Group T6A — Fixed and variable resistors; Capacitors; Inductors; Fuses; Switches; Batteries

### T6A01
**What electrical component opposes the flow of current in a DC circuit?**
- A) Inductor
- **B) Resistor** ✅
- C) Inverter
- D) Transformer

> A resistor opposes the flow of current in a circuit. Resistance is measured in ohms, and resistors are used to control current levels, divide voltages, and set operating points in circuits. They convert electrical energy to heat.

### T6A02
**What type of component is often used as an adjustable volume control?**
- A) Fixed resistor
- B) Power resistor
- **C) Potentiometer** ✅
- D) Transformer

> A potentiometer is a variable resistor with a knob or slider, which makes it the classic volume control. Turning it changes how much of the audio signal is passed along to the amplifier. Fixed resistors and power resistors can't be adjusted, and a transformer changes AC voltage levels rather than offering a smooth adjustable control.

### T6A03
**What electrical parameter is controlled by a potentiometer?**
- A) Inductance
- **B) Resistance** ✅
- C) Capacitance
- D) Field strength

> A potentiometer is an adjustable resistor, so the electrical parameter it controls is resistance. Moving its wiper along the resistive element changes the resistance between the wiper and each end. Inductance and capacitance are adjusted with variable inductors and variable capacitors, and a potentiometer doesn't control field strength.

### T6A04
**What electrical component stores energy in an electric field?**
- A) Resistor
- **B) Capacitor** ✅
- C) Inductor
- D) Diode

> A capacitor stores energy in an electric field between its plates. When voltage is applied, charge builds up on the plates, storing energy. Capacitors are used for filtering, coupling, timing, and energy storage throughout electronics.

### T6A05
**What type of electrical component consists of conductive surfaces separated by an insulator?**
- A) Resistor
- B) Potentiometer
- C) Oscillator
- **D) Capacitor** ✅

> A capacitor is made of conductive surfaces, or plates, separated by an insulator called the dielectric. Charge collects on the plates, and the energy is stored in the electric field between them. Resistors and potentiometers oppose current flow, and an oscillator is a circuit that generates a signal, not a component built this way.

### T6A06
**What type of electrical component stores energy in a magnetic field?**
- A) Resistor
- B) Capacitor
- **C) Inductor** ✅
- D) Diode

> An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field. When current flows through the coil, a magnetic field forms around it, storing energy. This is the fundamental operating principle of inductors, transformers, and many RF circuits.

### T6A07
**What electrical component is typically constructed as a coil of wire?**
- A) Transistor
- B) Capacitor
- C) Diode
- **D) Inductor** ✅

> An inductor is typically constructed as a coil of wire. The coil shape concentrates the magnetic field and increases the inductance. Adding a ferromagnetic core (like iron) further increases the inductance.

### T6A08
**What is the function of an SPDT switch?**
- A) A single circuit is opened or closed
- B) Two circuits are opened or closed
- **C) A single circuit is switched between one of two other circuits** ✅
- D) Two circuits are each switched between one of two other circuits

> SPDT stands for single-pole, double-throw. Single pole means one circuit is being switched, and double throw means that circuit can be connected to either of two other circuits. Choice A describes a single-pole, single-throw on-off switch, and choices B and D describe double-pole switches, which handle two circuits at once.

### T6A09
**What type of switch is represented by component 3 in figure T-2?**
- **A) Single-pole single-throw** ✅
- B) Single-pole double-throw
- C) Double-pole single-throw
- D) Double-pole double-throw

> Component 3 in figure T-2 represents a single-pole single-throw (SPST) switch. SPST is the simplest switch type — it either connects or disconnects a single circuit path. One pole (connection point), one throw (position).

### T6A10
**Which of the following battery chemistries is rechargeable?**
- A) Nickel-metal hydride
- B) Lithium-ion
- C) Lead-acid
- **D) All these choices are correct** ✅

> Nickel-metal hydride, lithium-ion, and lead-acid are all rechargeable, or secondary, battery chemistries. Nickel-metal hydride is common in rechargeable AA and AAA cells, lithium-ion powers handheld radios and phones, and lead-acid is the car battery that's also popular for station backup power. Since all three are rechargeable, the answer is all these choices are correct.

### T6A11
**Which of the following battery chemistries is not rechargeable?**
- A) Nickel-cadmium
- **B) Carbon-zinc** ✅
- C) Lead-acid
- D) Lithium-ion

> Carbon-zinc cells are primary, or disposable, batteries and are not designed to be recharged. Trying to recharge them can make them leak or rupture. Nickel-cadmium, lead-acid, and lithium-ion are all rechargeable chemistries.

## Group T6B — Semiconductors: basic principles and applications of solid-state devices, diodes and transistors; Gain

### T6B01
**Which is true about forward voltage drop in a diode?**
- **A) It is lower in some diode types than in others** ✅
- B) It is proportional to peak inverse voltage
- C) It indicates that the diode is defective
- D) It has no impact on the voltage delivered to the load

> Forward voltage drop depends on the diode type. A silicon diode drops about 0.7 volts, while germanium and Schottky diodes drop less, around 0.2 to 0.3 volts. That drop is normal and doesn't mean the diode is defective. It also isn't proportional to peak inverse voltage, and it does reduce the voltage delivered to the load.

### T6B02
**What electronic component allows current to flow in only one direction?**
- A) Resistor
- B) Fuse
- **C) Diode** ✅
- D) Driven element

> A diode works like a one-way valve, letting current flow in only one direction, from anode to cathode, and blocking it the other way. A resistor opposes current in both directions, and a fuse opens to protect a circuit when the current gets too high. A driven element is part of an antenna, not a circuit component.

### T6B03
**Which of these components can be used as an electronic switch?**
- A) Varistor
- B) Potentiometer
- **C) Transistor** ✅
- D) Thermistor

> A transistor's main function is to amplify or switch electronic signals. It's the fundamental building block of modern electronics — every computer chip contains billions of transistors. In amateur radio, transistors amplify RF signals in receivers and transmitters.

### T6B04
**Which of the following components can consist of three regions of semiconductor material?**
- A) Alternator
- **B) Transistor** ✅
- C) Triode
- D) Pentode

> A transistor consists of three regions of semiconductor material. In a bipolar junction transistor (BJT), these are the emitter, base, and collector. In a field-effect transistor (FET), they're the source, gate, and drain.

### T6B05
**What type of transistor has a gate, drain, and source?**
- A) Varistor
- **B) Field-effect** ✅
- C) Hall-effect
- D) Bipolar junction

> A field-effect transistor (FET) has a gate, drain, and source. This distinguishes it from a bipolar transistor, which has a base, collector, and emitter. FETs are widely used in receivers because of their high input impedance.

### T6B06
**How is the cathode lead of a semiconductor diode often marked on the package?**
- A) With the word "cathode"
- **B) With a stripe** ✅
- C) With the letter C
- D) With the letter K

> The cathode end of a semiconductor diode is usually marked with a stripe or band around the package. The stripe matches the bar at the cathode end of the diode's schematic symbol. Diodes are usually too small to print the word cathode or a letter on them, so the stripe is the standard marking.

### T6B07
**What causes a light-emitting diode (LED) to emit light?**
- **A) Forward current** ✅
- B) Reverse current
- C) Capacitively-coupled RF signal
- D) Inductively-coupled RF signal

> An LED emits light due to forward current flowing through the semiconductor junction. When electrons cross the junction and recombine, they release energy as photons (light). The color depends on the semiconductor material used.

### T6B08
**What does the abbreviation FET stand for?**
- A) Frequency Emission Transmitter
- B) Fast Electron Transistor
- C) Free Electron Transmitter
- **D) Field Effect Transistor** ✅

> FET stands for field effect transistor. The voltage on its gate creates an electric field that controls the current flowing between the source and the drain. The other choices are made-up phrases, and a FET is a transistor, not a transmitter.

### T6B09
**What are the names for the electrodes of a diode?**
- A) Plus and minus
- B) Source and drain
- **C) Anode and cathode** ✅
- D) Gate and base

> The two electrodes of a diode are the anode and the cathode. When the diode is forward biased, current flows from anode to cathode. Source, drain, and gate are field-effect transistor terminals, and base belongs to a bipolar transistor. Plus and minus describe polarity, not the names of the electrodes.

### T6B10
**Which of the following can provide power gain?**
- A) Transformer
- **B) Transistor** ✅
- C) Reactor
- D) Resistor

> A transistor can provide power gain: a small input signal controls a larger current drawn from the power supply, so the output can have more power than the input. A transformer can raise voltage, but only by lowering current, so it never adds power. Resistors and reactors only oppose current and can't add power to a signal.

### T6B11
**What does the term gain mean in amplifiers?**
- A) The output signal voltage relative to the input signal voltage
- B) The output signal current relative to the input signal current
- C) The output signal power relative to the input signal power
- **D) All these choices are correct** ✅

> In amplifiers, the term "gain" refers to the ratio of the output signal to the input signal — it can describe voltage gain, current gain, or power gain. All of these are valid meanings of gain in the context of amplifiers.

### T6B12
**What are the names of the electrodes of a bipolar junction transistor?**
- A) Signal, bias, power
- **B) Emitter, base, collector** ✅
- C) Input, output, supply
- D) Pole one, pole two, output

> The three electrodes of a bipolar junction transistor are the emitter, base, and collector. The base controls the current flow between the emitter and collector. In an NPN transistor, a small current into the base allows a much larger current to flow from collector to emitter, and that's how transistors amplify.

## Group T6D — Component functions: rectifiers, relays, voltage regulators, meters, indicators, integrated circuits, transformers; Resonant circuit; Shielding

### T6D01
**Which of the following devices or circuits changes an alternating current into a varying direct current signal?**
- A) Transformer
- **B) Rectifier** ✅
- C) Amplifier
- D) Reflector

> A rectifier changes alternating current into a varying, or pulsating, direct current by letting current flow in only one direction, usually with diodes. A transformer changes one AC voltage to another, but its output is still AC. An amplifier increases a signal's strength, and a reflector is part of an antenna.

### T6D02
**What is a relay?**
- **A) An electrically-controlled switch** ✅
- B) A current-controlled amplifier
- C) An inverting amplifier
- D) A pass transistor

> A relay is an electrically-controlled switch: an electromagnet that physically opens or closes a set of contacts. When current flows through the relay's coil, the magnetic field pulls the contacts closed, or open. Relays let a small control signal switch a larger power circuit. A relay is not an amplifier or a pass transistor.

### T6D03
**Which of the following is a reason to use shielded wire?**
- A) To decrease the resistance of DC power connections
- B) To increase the current carrying capability of the wire
- **C) To prevent coupling of unwanted signals to or from the wire** ✅
- D) To reduce receiver overload

> Shielded wire prevents unwanted signals from coupling to or from the wire. The shield acts as a barrier to external RF fields, keeping interference out and keeping the wire from radiating like an antenna. It doesn't lower DC resistance or raise current-carrying capability, and it isn't the cure for receiver overload, which comes from strong signals reaching the receiver's input.

### T6D04
**Which of the following displays an electrical quantity as a numeric value?**
- A) Potentiometer
- B) Transistor
- **C) Meter** ✅
- D) Relay

> A meter, such as a digital voltmeter or multimeter, displays an electrical quantity like voltage, current, or resistance as a numeric value. A potentiometer is a variable resistor, a transistor is a switch or amplifier, and a relay is an electrically controlled switch. None of those displays anything.

### T6D05
**What type of circuit controls the amount of voltage from a power supply?**
- **A) Regulator** ✅
- B) Oscillator
- C) Filter
- D) Phase inverter

> A voltage regulator controls the amount of voltage from a power supply. It holds the output steady even when the input voltage or the load changes. An oscillator generates a signal, a filter removes unwanted frequencies or ripple, and a phase inverter flips a signal's phase, but none of them sets the output voltage.

### T6D06
**What component changes 120 V AC power to a lower AC voltage for other uses?**
- A) Variable capacitor
- **B) Transformer** ✅
- C) Transistor
- D) Diode

> A transformer changes 120 volt AC power to a lower AC voltage. It has two coils wound on a shared core. AC in the primary coil creates a changing magnetic field that induces a voltage in the secondary, and the ratio of turns sets the output voltage. A variable capacitor, a transistor, and a diode can't step AC voltage down this way, though a diode is often used afterward to turn the AC into DC.

### T6D07
**Which of the following is commonly used as a visual indicator?**
- **A) LED** ✅
- B) FET
- C) Zener diode
- D) All these choices are correct

> An LED, or light-emitting diode, is the standard visual indicator for power, transmit, and status lights because it's bright, efficient, and long-lasting. A FET is a transistor used for switching and amplifying, and a Zener diode regulates voltage, and neither gives off light. That also rules out all these choices are correct.

### T6D08
**Which of the following is combined with an inductor to make a resonant circuit?**
- A) Resistor
- B) Zener diode
- C) Potentiometer
- **D) Capacitor** ✅

> An inductor combined with a capacitor makes a resonant, or tuned, circuit. At the resonant frequency, energy swaps back and forth between the inductor's magnetic field and the capacitor's electric field. A resistor or potentiometer only uses up energy, and a Zener diode is used to regulate voltage, so none of them can form a resonant circuit with an inductor.

### T6D09
**What is the name of a device that combines several semiconductors and other components into one package?**
- A) Transducer
- B) Multi-pole relay
- **C) Integrated circuit** ✅
- D) Transformer

> An integrated circuit, or IC, combines many semiconductors such as transistors and diodes, along with other components such as resistors, into one package. A transducer converts one form of energy into another, the way a microphone does. A multi-pole relay is a switch, and a transformer is a pair of magnetically coupled coils.

### T6D10
**What is the function of component 2 in figure T-1?**
- A) Give off light when current flows through it
- B) Supply electrical energy
- **C) Control the flow of current** ✅
- D) Convert electrical energy into radio waves

> Component 2 in figure T-1 is a transistor, drawn as a circle containing a bar with two angled leads, one with an arrow. Its function is to control the flow of current: a small signal through the resistor into its base controls a larger current through the lamp. The lamp, component 3, is what gives off light, and the battery, component 4, supplies the electrical energy. Turning electrical energy into radio waves is the antenna's job.

### T6D11
**Which of the following is a resonant or tuned circuit?**
- **A) An inductor and a capacitor in series or parallel** ✅
- B) A linear voltage regulator
- C) A resistor circuit used for reducing standing wave ratio
- D) A circuit designed to provide high-fidelity audio

> A resonant, or tuned, circuit is an inductor and a capacitor connected either in series or in parallel. It responds strongly at one frequency, which is how a radio picks out one station. A voltage regulator holds voltage steady, and resistor networks and audio circuits don't have the inductor and capacitor pair that makes resonance possible.
