## Group T5A — Current and voltage: terminology and units, conductors and insulators, alternating and direct current

### T5A01
**Electrical current is measured in which of the following units?**
- A) Volts
- B) Watts
- C) Ohms
- **D) Amperes** ✅

> Electrical current is measured in amperes (amps). One ampere equals one coulomb of charge flowing past a point per second. The symbol for current is I, and the instrument that measures it is an ammeter.

### T5A02
**Electrical power is measured in which of the following units?**
- A) Volts
- **B) Watts** ✅
- C) Watt-hours
- D) Amperes

> Electrical power is measured in watts, which tell you how fast electrical energy is being used. Volts measure electrical pressure, and amperes measure current. Watt-hours are the tempting wrong answer: they measure energy, which is power multiplied by time, not power itself.

### T5A03
**What is the term for the flow of electrons in an electric circuit?**
- A) Voltage
- B) Amperes per second
- C) Volts per second
- **D) Current** ✅

> The flow of electrons in an electric circuit is called current. Current is what moves through wires and components, doing useful work. It's measured in amperes and represented by the letter I in equations.

### T5A04
**What term describes the number of times per second that an alternating current makes a complete cycle?**
- A) Pulse rate
- B) Wave number
- **C) Frequency** ✅
- D) Wavelength

> Frequency describes the number of times per second that an alternating current makes a complete cycle. One complete cycle per second equals one hertz (Hz). AC power in the US cycles at 60 Hz — meaning 60 complete cycles per second.

### T5A05
**A difference in which of the following causes electron flow?**
- **A) Voltage** ✅
- B) Ampere-hours
- C) Capacitance
- D) Inductance

> A difference in electrical potential (voltage) causes electrons to flow. When there's a voltage difference between two points, electrons are pushed from the negative terminal toward the positive terminal. No voltage difference, no current flow.

### T5A06
**What is the unit of frequency?**
- **A) Hertz** ✅
- B) Henry
- C) Farad
- D) Epicycles per second

> The unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz). One hertz equals one cycle per second. The unit is named after Heinrich Hertz, who first proved the existence of radio waves. The henry is the unit of inductance, the farad is the unit of capacitance, and epicycles per second is not a real unit.

### T5A07
**Why are metals generally good conductors of electricity?**
- A) They have relatively high density
- **B) They have many free electrons** ✅
- C) They have many free protons
- D) They have relatively high Young’s modulus values

> Metals are good conductors because they have many free electrons that can move easily from atom to atom. These loosely bound electrons in the outer shells of metal atoms create a "sea" of mobile charge carriers. Materials with tightly bound electrons (like glass or rubber) are insulators.

### T5A08
**Which of the following is a good electrical insulator?**
- A) Sea water
- **B) Glass** ✅
- C) Stainless steel
- D) Graphite

> Glass is a good electrical insulator — it strongly resists the flow of electric current because its electrons are tightly bound to their atoms. Sea water, stainless steel, and graphite all conduct electricity to varying degrees.

### T5A09
**Which of the following describes alternating current?**
- A) Current that alternates between a positive direction and zero
- B) Current that alternates between a negative direction and zero
- **C) Current that alternates between positive and negative directions** ✅
- D) All these answers are correct

> Alternating current reverses direction, swinging back and forth between positive and negative directions, like the 60 hertz power from a wall outlet. Current that only goes between a positive direction and zero, or between a negative direction and zero, never reverses, so it is pulsating direct current, not AC. Because those two choices are wrong, all these answers are correct is wrong too.

### T5A10
**Which term describes the rate at which electrical energy is used?**
- A) Resistance
- B) Current
- **C) Power** ✅
- D) Voltage

> Power is the rate at which electrical energy is used, and it's measured in watts. A 100 watt bulb uses energy twice as fast as a 50 watt bulb. Resistance opposes current, current is the flow of electrons, and voltage is the pressure that pushes them, but none of those is a rate of energy use.

### T5A11
**What type of current flow is opposed by resistance?**
- A) Direct current
- B) Alternating current
- C) RF current
- **D) All these choices are correct** ✅

> Resistance opposes every kind of current flow: direct current, alternating current, and radio frequency current. A resistor turns some of the current's energy into heat no matter which way the current flows or how fast it alternates. Since each of the first three choices is correct, the answer is all these choices are correct.

## Group T5B — Math for electronics: conversion of electrical units, decibels

### T5B01
**How many milliamperes is 1.5 amperes?**
- A) 0.0000015 milliamperes
- B) 0.0015 milliamperes
- **C) 1500 milliamperes** ✅
- D) 1,500,000 milliamperes

> 1.5 amperes equals 1,500 milliamperes. The prefix "milli" means one-thousandth, so to convert amps to milliamps, multiply by 1,000. Going the other way, divide milliamps by 1,000 to get amps.

### T5B02
**Which is equal to 1,500,000 hertz?**
- **A) 1500 kHz** ✅
- B) 1500 MHz
- C) 15 GHz
- D) 150 kHz

> Kilo means one thousand, so divide 1,500,000 hertz by 1000 to get 1500 kilohertz, which is also 1.5 megahertz. 1500 megahertz is one thousand times too large, and 15 gigahertz is larger still. 150 kilohertz is ten times too small.

### T5B03
**Which is equal to one kilovolt?**
- A) One one-thousandth of a volt
- B) One hundred volts
- **C) One thousand volts** ✅
- D) One million volts

> The prefix kilo means one thousand, so one kilovolt is one thousand volts. One one-thousandth of a volt is a millivolt, and one million volts is a megavolt. One hundred volts simply isn't what kilo means.

### T5B04
**Which is equal to one microvolt?**
- **A) One one-millionth of a volt** ✅
- B) One million volts
- C) One thousand kilovolts
- D) One one-thousandth of a volt

> The prefix micro means one millionth, so one microvolt is one one-millionth of a volt. That's the size of the weak signals that arrive at a receiver's antenna input. One one-thousandth of a volt is a millivolt, and one million volts and one thousand kilovolts are both a megavolt, the opposite end of the scale.

### T5B05
**Which is equal to 500 milliwatts?**
- A) 5 watts
- **B) 0.5 watts** ✅
- C) 500,000 watts
- D) 500,000,000 watts

> 500 milliwatts equals 0.5 watts. "Milli" means one-thousandth, so 500 milliwatts = 500/1000 = 0.5 watts. Easy conversion — just move the decimal point three places.

### T5B06
**Which is equal to 3000 milliamperes?**
- A) 0.003 amperes
- B) 0.3 amperes
- C) 3,000,000 amperes
- **D) 3 amperes** ✅

> Milli means one-thousandth, so divide milliamperes by 1000 to get amperes: 3000 milliamperes divided by 1000 is 3 amperes. 0.003 amperes and 0.3 amperes are far too small. 3,000,000 amperes comes from multiplying by 1000 instead of dividing.

### T5B07
**Which is equal to 3.525 MHz?**
- A) 0.003525 kHz
- B) 35.25 kHz
- **C) 3525 kHz** ✅
- D) 3,525,000 kHz

> Mega is one thousand times kilo, so to go from megahertz to kilohertz, multiply by 1000: 3.525 megahertz times 1000 is 3525 kilohertz. That frequency is in the 80 meter band. 0.003525 kilohertz and 35.25 kilohertz are much too small, and 3,525,000 kilohertz is one thousand times too large.

### T5B08
**Which is equal to 1,000,000 picofarads?**
- A) 0.001 microfarads
- **B) 1 microfarad** ✅
- C) 1000 microfarads
- D) 1,000,000,000 microfarads

> Pico means one trillionth and micro means one millionth, so there are one million picofarads in one microfarad. That makes 1,000,000 picofarads exactly 1 microfarad. 0.001 microfarads is only 1000 picofarads, and the two larger choices are far too big.

### T5B09
**Which decibel value most closely represents a power increase from 5 watts to 10 watts?**
- A) 2 dB
- **B) 3 dB** ✅
- C) 5 dB
- D) 10 dB

> Doubling power is an increase of about 3 decibels. Going from 5 watts to 10 watts doubles the power, so the answer is 3 dB. The exact value is 10 times the logarithm of 2, which is 3.01 dB. A 10 dB increase would mean ten times the power, all the way to 50 watts.

### T5B10
**Which decibel value most closely represents a power decrease from 12 watts to 3 watts?**
- A) -1 dB
- B) -3 dB
- **C) -6 dB** ✅
- D) -9 dB

> Cutting power in half is a change of minus 3 decibels. Going from 12 watts to 6 watts is one halving, minus 3 dB, and going from 6 watts to 3 watts is another, for a total of minus 6 dB. Minus 3 dB would stop at 6 watts, and minus 9 dB would take you all the way down to 1.5 watts.

### T5B11
**Which decibel value represents a power increase from 20 watts to 200 watts?**
- **A) 10 dB** ✅
- B) 12 dB
- C) 18 dB
- D) 28 dB

> Multiplying power by ten is an increase of 10 decibels. 200 watts is exactly ten times 20 watts, so the increase is 10 dB. The other choices are too large: even 12 dB would be about 16 times the power.

### T5B12
**Which is equal to 28400 kHz?**
- A) 28.400 kHz
- B) 2.800 MHz
- C) 284.00 MHz
- **D) 28.400 MHz** ✅

> To convert kilohertz to megahertz, divide by 1000: 28,400 kilohertz divided by 1000 is 28.400 megahertz, in the 10 meter band. 28.400 kilohertz shrinks the number without changing to the larger unit, so it's one thousand times too small. 2.800 megahertz is about ten times too small, and 284.00 megahertz is ten times too large.

### T5B13
**Which is equal to 2425 MHz?**
- A) 0.002425 GHz
- B) 24.25 GHz
- **C) 2.425 GHz** ✅
- D) 242.5 GHz

> 2425 MHz equals 2.425 GHz. To convert MHz to GHz, divide by 1,000. This frequency is near the 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi band and falls within the amateur 13-centimeter band.

## Group T5C — Capacitance and inductance terminology and units; Radio frequency definition and units; Impedance definition and units; Calculating power

### T5C01
**What describes the ability to store energy in an electric field?**
- A) Inductance
- B) Resistance
- C) Frequency
- **D) Capacitance** ✅

> Capacitance describes the ability to store energy in an electric field. A capacitor stores charge on its plates, and the amount of charge it can store per volt is its capacitance. Don't confuse this with inductance (energy in a magnetic field).

### T5C02
**What is the unit of capacitance?**
- **A) Farad** ✅
- B) Ohm
- C) Volt
- D) Henry

> The unit of capacitance is the farad (F). Named after Michael Faraday, most practical capacitors are measured in microfarads (µF) or picofarads (pF), since one farad is an enormous amount of capacitance.

### T5C03
**What describes the ability to store energy in a magnetic field?**
- A) Admittance
- B) Capacitance
- C) Resistance
- **D) Inductance** ✅

> Inductance describes the ability to store energy in a magnetic field. An inductor (coil) creates a magnetic field when current flows through it, and that field stores energy. Inductance opposes changes in current.

### T5C04
**What is the unit of inductance?**
- A) Coulomb
- B) Farad
- **C) Henry** ✅
- D) Ohm

> The unit of inductance is the henry (H). Named after Joseph Henry, inductors are typically measured in millihenrys (mH) or microhenrys (µH). One henry is a lot of inductance — most practical inductors are much smaller.

### T5C05
**What is the unit of impedance?**
- A) Volt
- B) Ampere
- C) Coulomb
- **D) Ohm** ✅

> The unit of impedance is the ohm (Ω) — the same unit used for pure resistance. Impedance combines resistance and reactance (from capacitors and inductors) into a single measure of opposition to current flow in AC circuits.

### T5C06
**What is the abbreviation for kilohertz?**
- **A) kHz** ✅
- B) khz
- C) KHz
- D) KHZ

> The abbreviation for kilohertz is kHz — lowercase k, uppercase H, lowercase z. The lowercase k indicates the metric prefix "kilo" (1,000), while Hz is the unit abbreviation for hertz. KHz, khz, and KHZ are all incorrect.

### T5C07
**What is the abbreviation for megahertz?**
- A) mHz
- B) mHZ
- C) Mhz
- **D) MHz** ✅

> The abbreviation for megahertz is MHz — uppercase M, uppercase H, lowercase z. The uppercase M indicates the metric prefix "mega" (1,000,000). Be careful: mHz (lowercase m) would mean millihertz, which is a completely different order of magnitude.

### T5C08
**What formula is used to calculate electrical power (P) in a DC circuit?**
- **A) P = I x E** ✅
- B) P = E / I
- C) P = I2 x E
- D) P = I / E

> The formula for electrical power in a DC circuit is P = I × E (power equals current times voltage). This is the most basic power formula. From this, you can derive P = I²R and P = E²/R using Ohm's Law substitutions.

### T5C09
**How much power is delivered by a voltage of 13.8 volts DC and a current of 10 amperes?**
- **A) 138 watts** ✅
- B) 0.7 watts
- C) 23.8 watts
- D) 3.8 watts

> Power equals current times voltage. 10 amperes times 13.8 volts is 138 watts. The wrong choices come from adding, subtracting, or dividing instead of multiplying: 13.8 plus 10 is 23.8, 13.8 minus 10 is 3.8, and 10 divided by 13.8 is about 0.7.

### T5C10
**How much power is delivered by a voltage of 12 volts DC and a current of 2.5 amperes?**
- A) 4.8 watts
- **B) 30 watts** ✅
- C) 14.5 watts
- D) 0.208 watts

> Power equals current times voltage: 2.5 amperes times 12 volts is 30 watts. The other choices come from dividing or adding instead. 12 divided by 2.5 is 4.8, 12 plus 2.5 is 14.5, and 2.5 divided by 12 is about 0.208.

### T5C11
**How much current is required to deliver 120 watts at a voltage of 12 volts DC?**
- A) 0.1 amperes
- **B) 10 amperes** ✅
- C) 12 amperes
- D) 132 amperes

> Rearrange the power formula so that current equals power divided by voltage. 120 watts divided by 12 volts is 10 amperes. 0.1 amperes comes from dividing the wrong way, 12 by 120, and 132 amperes comes from adding the two numbers.

### T5C12
**What is impedance?**
- **A) The opposition to AC current flow** ✅
- B) The inverse of resistance
- C) The inverse of reactance
- D) The power handling capability of a component

> Impedance is the opposition to AC current flow, combining both resistance and reactance. Resistance opposes both AC and DC, while the reactance of capacitors and inductors depends on frequency, so impedance describes the total opposition in an AC circuit. It's measured in ohms, just like resistance. It is not the inverse of resistance or reactance, and it has nothing to do with power handling.

## Group T5D — Ohm's Law; Series and parallel circuits

### T5D01
**What formula is used to calculate current in a circuit?**
- A) I = E x R
- **B) I = E / R** ✅
- C) I = E2 x R
- D) I = E2 / R

> The formula for current is I = E / R (current equals voltage divided by resistance). This is Ohm's Law rearranged to solve for current. If you know the voltage across a circuit and its resistance, you can calculate how much current flows.

### T5D02
**What formula is used to calculate voltage in a circuit?**
- **A) E = I x R** ✅
- B) E = I / R
- C) E = I2 x R
- D) E = I2 / R

> The formula for voltage is E = I × R (voltage equals current times resistance). This is the fundamental form of Ohm's Law. If 2 amps flow through 50 ohms, the voltage is 100 volts.

### T5D03
**What formula is used to calculate resistance in a circuit?**
- A) R = E x I
- **B) R = E / I** ✅
- C) R = E + I
- D) R = E - I

> The formula for resistance is R = E / I (resistance equals voltage divided by current). This is Ohm's Law solved for resistance. If you apply 12 volts and measure 3 amps, the resistance is 4 ohms.

### T5D04
**What is the resistance of a circuit in which a current of 3 amperes flows when connected to 90 volts?**
- A) 3 ohms
- **B) 30 ohms** ✅
- C) 1/30 ohm
- D) 270 ohms

> Using R = E / I: 90 volts ÷ 3 amps = 30 ohms. Ohm's Law makes this straightforward — divide the voltage by the current to find the resistance. Always make sure your units are consistent (volts, amps, ohms).

### T5D05
**What is the resistance of a circuit for which the applied voltage is 12 volts and the current flow is 1.5 amperes?**
- A) 18 ohms
- B) 0.125 ohms
- **C) 8 ohms** ✅
- D) 13.5 ohms

> Resistance equals voltage divided by current. 12 volts divided by 1.5 amperes is 8 ohms. Multiplying gives 18, dividing the wrong way gives 0.125, and adding gives 13.5, and all of those are wrong.

### T5D06
**What is the resistance of a circuit that draws 4 amperes from a 12-volt source?**
- **A) 3 ohms** ✅
- B) 16 ohms
- C) 48 ohms
- D) 8 ohms

> Resistance equals voltage divided by current: 12 volts divided by 4 amperes is 3 ohms. Multiplying gives 48 and adding gives 16, which are both wrong. 8 ohms is what you'd get by subtracting.

### T5D07
**What is the current in a circuit with an applied voltage of 120 volts and a resistance of 80 ohms?**
- A) 9600 amperes
- B) 200 amperes
- C) 0.667 amperes
- **D) 1.5 amperes** ✅

> Using I = E / R: 120 volts ÷ 80 ohms = 1.5 amps. Divide the voltage by the resistance to find the current. This is a common household example — 120V through a moderate resistance.

### T5D08
**What is the current through a 100-ohm resistor connected across 200 volts?**
- A) 20,000 amperes
- B) 0.5 amperes
- **C) 2 amperes** ✅
- D) 100 amperes

> Current equals voltage divided by resistance: 200 volts divided by 100 ohms is 2 amperes. Dividing the wrong way, 100 by 200, gives 0.5 amperes. Multiplying gives 20,000 amperes.

### T5D09
**What is the current through a 24-ohm resistor connected across 240 volts?**
- A) 2400 amperes
- B) 0.1 amperes
- **C) 10 amperes** ✅
- D) 5760 amperes

> Using I = E / R: 240 volts ÷ 24 ohms = 10 amps. Divide the voltage by the resistance. 240V is a common higher-voltage household circuit (used for dryers, ovens, etc.).

### T5D10
**What is the voltage across a 2-ohm resistor if a current of 0.5 amperes flows through it?**
- **A) 1 volt** ✅
- B) 0.25 volts
- C) 2.5 volts
- D) 1.5 volts

> Voltage equals current times resistance: 0.5 amperes times 2 ohms is 1 volt. 0.25 volts comes from dividing 0.5 by 2, 2.5 volts comes from adding the numbers, and 1.5 volts comes from subtracting them.

### T5D11
**What is the voltage across a 10-ohm resistor if a current of 1 ampere flows through it?**
- A) 1 volt
- **B) 10 volts** ✅
- C) 11 volts
- D) 9 volts

> Voltage equals current times resistance: 1 ampere times 10 ohms is 10 volts. Adding the numbers gives 11 volts and subtracting gives 9 volts, and neither is how Ohm's law works.

### T5D12
**What is the voltage across a 10-ohm resistor if a current of 2 amperes flows through it?**
- A) 8 volts
- B) 0.2 volts
- C) 12 volts
- **D) 20 volts** ✅

> Voltage equals current times resistance: 2 amperes times 10 ohms is 20 volts. Dividing 2 by 10 gives 0.2 volts, adding gives 12 volts, and subtracting gives 8 volts, and all of those are wrong.

### T5D13
**In which type of circuit is the current always the same through all components?**
- **A) Series** ✅
- B) Parallel
- C) Resonant
- D) Branch

> In a series circuit, the current is always the same through all components. There's only one path for current to flow, so the same current passes through every component. Voltage divides among the components, but current is identical everywhere.

### T5D14
**In which type of circuit is the voltage always the same across all components?**
- A) Series
- **B) Parallel** ✅
- C) Resonant
- D) Branch

> In a parallel circuit, the voltage is always the same across all components. Each component connects directly across the power source, so they all see the same voltage. Current divides among the branches, but voltage is identical across all of them.
